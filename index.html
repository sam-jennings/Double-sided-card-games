The material

Every card has two faces.

A normal card hides its identity behind a shared back. These cards have no back — each one carries a different sign on each side, and only you decide which face the table sees.

Showing a face is a move. Hiding a face is a move. In one game the hidden side is your bluff; in another it's the poison you're forced to swallow; in a third it's a road turned to point the other way.

Tap the card. What it hides is the whole game.

The maths

Every pair, exactly once.

Nine signs can be paired 36 ways — and the deck is those 36 pairings, each printed on exactly one card. No duplicates, no gaps.

That's why the deck can be a route map, a betting track, a boss fight or a bluffing table: whatever two signs a game needs to connect, there is one — and only one — card that does it.

The vocabulary

The flip side: nine signs.

Every game reads the same nine signs differently — a Crown rules one game and builds the board in another. The pip is the rank; the picture is the story.

The paper deck

Print your own deck.

You don't have to wait for a printed copy to play any of this. The whole 36-card deck is a free PDF — nine A4 pages, nine cards to a sheet, ready for a home printer and a pair of scissors.

Because these cards have no back, the sheets print double-sided: a card's two signs land back to back on the same piece of stock.

  • A4 card stock, printed at 100% scale — turn “fit to page” off.
  • Page 1 is the legend. Print it single-sided, or skip it.
  • Pages 2–9 print double-sided, flipped on the long edge.
  • Hold a sheet up to the light to check alignment — the 4 mm frame forgives a little drift.
  • Cut on the dashed guides: nine cards a sheet, 36 in all. Standard 63.5 × 88 mm sleeves fit.
