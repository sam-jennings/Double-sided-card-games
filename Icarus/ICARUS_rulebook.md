# ICARUS

*for 2–4 players · ~10–15 minutes · ages 8+*

*ICARUS (ICK-uh-russ) flew on wings of wax — higher and higher, until the sun took them. Each card you dare to flip carries you further up. Land while you can and bank the climb, or reach for one more card and risk the fall. The deck shows you half of every gamble; the table's public record tells you the rest — if you're counting.*

## Components

The full master deck: **36 double-faced cards, 9 signs, every pair of signs on exactly one card, each sign on 8 cards**. Each sign has one fixed strength — Moon is always 1, Crown is always 9 (the pip on every face is its rank). Nothing else is needed. No subset: ICARUS uses all 9 signs.

> **Say aloud before the first game (rank canon):** "There are 9 signs. Each sign has one fixed strength — Crown is always 9, Moon is always 1. Every card pairs two different signs, so it always has two different strengths, one per face."

## Setup

1. **Shuffle by flipping random chunks**, so that which face lands up is randomised. (There is no card back — every card always shows one of its two faces.)
2. Split the deck into equal squared stacks, one per player: **18 / 12 / 9 cards at 2 / 3 / 4 players**. Each player's stack is their **tower**, placed upright in front of them. The top face of every tower is public — that's the deck's physics, and it's the game.
3. Clear a central space for the **Fallen field**: a face-up overlapping spread. Anyone may pick up and examine any card here, both sides, at any time — return it as found.
4. The player who most recently looked up at the sky goes first. Play proceeds clockwise.

## Your turn: one flight

A **flight** is a run of cards drawn from *your own* tower. Your flight builds a **row** in front of you — face-up, overlapping, left to right.

1. **Rise.** Take the top card of your tower — its showing face was already public — and **flip it** to reveal the pressed face.
   - If the revealed sign **matches any face-up sign already in your row**, you **fall**: your entire row and the fallen card go to the Fallen field. Your turn ends.
   - Otherwise, lay the card at the end of your row, revealed face up.
2. **Choose.** After each safe card:
   - **Rise again** — return to step 1, or
   - **Land** — bank your row (see *Landing*, below) and end your turn.

The first card of a flight can never make you fall — your row is empty. Every flight is at least one card.

If your tower runs out mid-flight, you land automatically, and pay no toll.

## Landing and the toll

When you land:

1. Move your row, intact and face-up as it lies, to your **banked clutches** — each flight stays together as its own small overlapping spread in front of you. Anyone may examine any banked card, both sides, at any time — return it as found.
2. **Pay the toll:** take the new top card of your tower and place it, as it lies, into the Fallen field. The night burns one card from your climb. (If your tower is empty, there is no toll.)

The toll is why you can't graze safely one card at a time: every landing costs a card that will never score.

## Soaring

A banked flight of **4 or more cards** is a **soar**. Bank it *sideways* (rotated 90°) — it scores **double**.

## Game end & scoring

The game ends when every tower is empty. Each banked card scores its **face-up rank**; sideways (soared) clutches score double. Highest total wins.

Tiebreakers, in order:

1. Most soaring flights banked.
2. Fewest of your cards in the Fallen field.
3. Share the victory.

## Counting the sky (the real game)

Every card that leaves a tower — banked, fallen, or burned as toll — is fully public and stays examinable. Because **every pair of signs exists on exactly one card**, the public record tells you exact odds:

Your tower's top shows Flame. Its pressed face is one of Flame's 8 partners. Your row shows Moon and Wave — those are your poisons. But the Flame–Moon card is lying in the Fallen field: Moon *cannot* be under this card. Only Wave can hurt you, and only if the Flame–Wave card hasn't surfaced elsewhere. What looked like a 2-in-8 risk may truly be 1-in-5 — or zero.

You never have to memorise anything: the Fallen field and every banked clutch may be re-read at any time.

## Edge cases & FAQ

**Q. The revealed sign matches a face-up sign in an *opponent's* row or banked clutch. Do I fall?**
No. Only your own current flight row is poison.

**Q. The card I drew has my poison sign on its *pressed* (now face-down) side. Do I fall?**
No. Only the *revealed* face matters. The face-down sides of your row are public knowledge but harmless.

**Q. My tower's top face shows a sign already in my row. Can I still rise?**
Yes — and it's actually *safer*: that card's hidden face can't be the same sign twice, so one of your poisons is impossible for this draw.

**Q. Can I land before drawing any card?**
No. A flight is at least one card. (It's always safe.)

**Q. I land and my tower has exactly one card left.**
Burn it as the toll. Your next turn is skipped — your tower is empty.

**Q. Do I have to announce my odds or my reasons?**
No. Count silently, count aloud, or don't count at all.

**Q. Someone is examining the Fallen field on my turn. Allowed?**
Yes, any time, as long as cards are returned exactly as found and play isn't held hostage. House rule if needed: examining waits until the current flip is resolved.

## Strategy notes — read after your first game

- **Two clean cards is a wage; four is a fortune.** A 2-card landing banks ~10 points. A soar doubles 4 cards — ~40 points. The whole game is deciding which flights are the ones to stretch.
- **The naive odds are a lie.** With three poisons showing, the untrained eye sees 3-in-8. The Fallen field often says otherwise — in both directions.
- **Late towers are open books.** Near the end, nearly every card is public. A counted final flight can be *provably* safe to four cards. Watch for it — and watch opponents who watch for it.
- **Spend your toll wisely.** Landing burns your next top card. If your top shows Crown, landing now throws a 9-or-partner into the night; sometimes that alone is a reason to keep flying.
- **High cards are sweet poison.** A revealed Crown is 9 points in your row — and one more sign that can kill the rest of your flight.

## Designer's variants

- **Fledgling (first game).** Skip the toll. Instead, each player gets **exactly 4 flights**; leftover tower cards never score. Simpler bookkeeping, same push — your flights are scarce instead of your cards.
- **Kestrel (memory).** The Fallen field is kept as a single squared *sideways* pile instead of an open spread — the record exists, but only in your head. Deliberate, symmetric hidden-face memory as the challenge; verify disputes at game end. *(Fills the collection's flagged memory-game gap.)*
- **Vulture (meaner, untested).** When a player falls, the player to their left may immediately take the single top card of the fallen row into their own banked clutches (face-up rank as it lies) before the rest hits the Fallen field.
