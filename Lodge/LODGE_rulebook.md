# LODGE

*for 2, 3 or 4 players · ~15–25 minutes (a match) · ages 8+*
*Adapted from the family memory game Beverbende (Beaver Gang) for the backless flip deck.*

> **State: In development.** Draft rules v0.1, written November 2026. Floor-simulated at 4 players (45,000 games, see the design notes); never played. See the design notes at the end for what the deck changes and what must be tested.

*A beaver lodge looks like a heap of sticks. The part that matters is underwater. Your lodge is four cards on the table: everyone can see the faces on top, but what counts against you is pressed underneath. Carry the lightest load when someone knocks.*

The core idea is the same as Beverbende: four cards in front of you, you only know some of them, and you swap, peek and steal to get your total as low as possible before someone knocks. The difference is the deck. Every card in your lodge **shows a sign to the whole table**, and a single flip can turn that public face into your score.

## Components

- The flip deck: **36 double-faced cards, 9 signs, every pair of signs on exactly one card, each sign on exactly 8 cards.** An index pip 1–9 is printed on both faces.
- Nothing else. Paper for keeping score over a match is optional.

**The Nine Signs, lowest to highest.** The pip is the sign's value. Low is good.

| Pip | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sign | Moon | Leaf | Wave | Flame | **Eye** | **Mask** | Key | **Star** | Crown |

The three signs in bold have **powers** (see *Powers*).

## Key terms

- Your **lodge** is the row of four cards in front of you.
- Each lodge card has a **shown face** (up, public) and a **pressed face** (against the table). **Only pressed faces score.**
- The **stock** is the draw pile. The **pond** is the discard pile.

## Setup

1. **Wash-shuffle** the whole deck: spread it on the table, swirl it, and turn cards over at random as you go, so you cannot track which face is up. There is no card back, so this is how you randomise faces. Gather it into a square **stock**.
2. **Deal each player a lodge of four cards** in a row in front of them. Slide each card off the top of the stock **without turning it over**: the face that was up stays up, and the face underneath goes straight to the table. Nobody has seen any pressed face.
3. Slide one more card off the stock, unturned, to start the **pond** beside it.
4. **First peek.** Each player lifts the near edge of their **two end cards**, one at a time, shields it with a hand, looks at the pressed face, and lowers it again without turning it. Remember them. Don't look at your two middle cards.

| Players | Lodge cards | Stock after dealing |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 8 | 27 |
| 3 | 12 | 23 |
| 4 | 16 | 19 |

The stock's top face is always visible. That is fine. You see half of the next card before you choose where to draw.

## Your turn

The youngest player starts. Play passes left. On your turn:

### 1. Draw one card

Choose one:

- **From the stock.** Take the top card. Everyone has already seen its upper face. Look at the other face privately.
- **From the pond.** Take the top card and look at both faces. A card revealed out of a lodge is fully public already; a discarded stock card's lower face may be known only to the player who discarded it. **A card taken from the pond must be kept.**

### 2. Keep it or discard it

**Keep.** Put the drawn card into your lodge in place of any one of your four cards. **You choose which face to press down.** Then pick up the card you replaced and **turn it over onto the pond**, so the face that was pressed is now showing. Everyone sees what you were carrying, and its other face is the one that sat on top in your lodge, so the pond card is now fully known.

**Discard** (stock cards only). Lay the card on the pond with **whichever face you like** showing. If that face is **Eye, Mask or Star**, you may use its power straight away. Only you know the face underneath.

### 3. Knock, if you want to

At the end of your turn you may **knock** on the table. Every other player takes **one more turn**. Then the round ends and everyone reckons.

**The stock running out also ends the round.** If you take the last stock card, finish your turn; the round ends immediately with no final turns.

## Powers

You only get a power by **discarding a card you drew from the stock**, with the power face showing on the pond. A pond card never gives a power to the player who takes it.

| Sign | Power | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Eye** (5) | **Peek** | Lift one of your own lodge cards, look at its pressed face, and lower it without turning it. |
| **Mask** (6) | **Swap** | Swap one of your lodge cards with one of a rival's. Slide them across the table: **don't lift or turn them**. Shown faces stay shown. Neither player looks. |
| **Star** (8) | **Turn** | Turn over **any one lodge card**, yours or a rival's, in its place. Its old pressed face now shows to everyone. Its old shown face is now pressed, and **that** is what it scores. |

Three cards carry two powers: Eye–Mask, Eye–Star and Mask–Star. Choose which face to put on the pond. That is the power you get.

> **Example: the Turn.** Ana's lodge shows a Crown on her third card. She placed it there herself, pressing its Moon. Ben draws a card from the stock showing Leaf and finds a Star underneath. He discards it Star-up and Turns Ana's third card: the Moon flips into view and the Crown is now pressed, counting 9 against her. Everyone saw the Moon, so everyone knows the Crown–Moon card is no longer anywhere else.

## Reckoning

When the round ends, every player turns over each lodge card in place and adds up the pips **now showing**. Those are the faces that were pressed. **The lowest total wins the round.**

**A match** is one round per player, with the first seat passing left each round. Add up your totals; the lowest match total wins. Ties are shared. If you want a single winner, the tied player who knocked fewer times wins.

## Edge cases & FAQ

**Can I look under my own lodge cards whenever I like?** No. You may only look at a pressed face during the first peek, or with an Eye.

**I swapped one of my cards away. Do I still know it?** Yes, if you remember it, and so might whoever you swapped with if you tell them nothing. Swaps move knowledge around; that is the game.

**Can I Turn or Swap a card after someone has knocked?** Yes. The knocker's lodge is still open until the reckoning.

**Can I Swap two rival cards with each other?** No. One of the two cards must be yours.

**Can I Turn a card in my own lodge?** Yes. It's the cleanest way to fix a card you peeked and found heavy.

**Can I keep a stock card instead of using its power?** Yes. A card with a power face is still a card. You may keep it with either face pressed.

**The pond's top card looks good, but I want a power. Can I take it and discard it?** No. A pond card must be kept.

**What if two players have no cards they want to change and nobody knocks?** In practice the leader knocks. The stock ending the round is the backstop, but see the open questions below.

## Strategy notes *(read after your first game)*

- **Pressing the low face shows the high face.** That is what makes you a Star target. Sometimes pressing a middling face and showing a low one is the safer keep.
- **Read the shown faces.** A card a player placed themselves almost always hides a lower sign than it shows. A card from the deal or a swap tells you nothing.
- **Count the registry.** Every pair exists once. When the Crown–Moon card has gone to the pond, no lodge holds it. Late in a round the shown faces plus the pond can pin down a rival's pressed face.
- **Swap blind, but not stupid.** Trade away a card you know is heavy for a rival card that shows a high sign: its owner probably pressed the low face.
- **Knock early when your lodge is solid and its shown faces are low.** That gives the table little to Turn.

## Designer's variants

- **Still Water (first game).** Ignore the Star power. Peek and Swap only. The game is close to plain Beverbende.
- **Dry Spell (harder).** No first peek. You start knowing nothing about your lodge.
- **Cold Knock.** If the knocker does not have the lowest total, they add 5 to their round score.

## Design notes *(not player-facing)*

### What the deck changes

Beverbende, Cabo and Golf all work with ordinary cards, so this adaptation must earn its place in the collection. Three things here can't happen with a card back:

1. **Every lodge card is half public.** The shown face rules out one sign for the pressed face, and the pair registry rules out more as the pond fills. Counting does real work late in a round. Early on, it does little: on its own a shown sign barely shifts the expected pressed pip (5.5 under a shown Moon, down to 4.5 under a shown Crown; computed over all 36 cards).
2. **The Turn makes your shown face a liability.** In Beverbende your hidden card can only be swapped. Here a rival can make the face you chose to show into your score. Every keep is a choice between a light score and a safe display.
3. **The stock shows half its next card.** You always know one face of what you'd draw.

This also fills a recorded gap. The steering notes say a **deliberate, verifiable memory game** is under-served (STRATA is the only other one, and it is still a concept). LODGE is that game in its most familiar, family-friendly form, with four cards in front of you and no large hand to fan.

### Physical-handling checklist

- **What can each player see?** Every shown face in every lodge, the pond's top face, and the stock's top face.
- **What can each player inspect?** Only the pressed faces they are entitled to: the first peek, an Eye peek, and both faces of a card they drew.
- **What is hidden, and is it really hidden?** Pressed lodge faces. They stay hidden because lodge cards are only ever slid, lifted at the near edge, or turned in place. The weak points are the deal (handled by sliding unturned) and the peek (a shielded lift, like any card game).
- **Is required state visible?** Yes. The score faces are hidden by design, and that is the memory challenge, not overhead. Everything else is face-up.
- **Can the table verify legality?** Yes. Replaced cards are revealed onto the pond, and the reckoning turns everything over. The only things you can't check are your own memories, which is the point.

### Verified facts (computed)

- 21 of 36 cards have at least one power face (Eye, Mask or Star). Three have two: Eye–Mask, Eye–Star and Mask–Star.
- Stock after dealing: 27 / 23 / 19 at 2 / 3 / 4 players.
- Best possible lodge: 4 (four Moons pressed; Moon is on 8 cards).

### Simulation at 4 players (November 2026)

[`lodge_sim.py`](lodge_sim.py) models these rules, including who knows which pressed face (and the public leak when you press the face the table saw on the stock). Two bots: a random bot, and a skilled bot that keeps low, uses powers for clear gains, swaps known-heavy cards for rival cards it can read, and knocks at an estimated 12 or less when it believes it leads. 5,000 games per row, turn cap 400, seed 1.

| Rules | Table | Turns per player | Ended by knock | Ended by stock | Stalls |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Base | 4 skilled | 2.1 (90th pct 3) | 100% | 0% | 0% |
| Base | 4 random | 5.5 (90th pct 8.8) | 87% | 13% | 0% |
| Pond recycle | 4 random | 5.8 | 99% | 1% | 0% |

- **The 19-card stock is not the limit.** Skilled rounds end by knock long before it runs out. Only random play reaches the bottom.
- **No stalls.** The pond-loop worry (open question 4) did not occur in 45,000 games, so the burn rule is not needed.
- **Skill gap is healthy.** One skilled bot among three random ones wins 88% of rounds (fair share 25%) and scores 10.7 against 20.5, about 1.9×.
- **Knocking early is not dominant.** Against three skilled bots that knock at 12, a seat that knocks at 6 wins 31% and one that knocks at the first chance wins 21%. *Cold Knock* changes these by about one point.
- **Powers are rare per round.** About 1.6 per round with skilled play (0.2 peeks, 0.7 swaps, 0.7 Turns), so the 58% power-card share does not drown the memory game. *Still Water* scores the same as base.
- **First-seat edge.** At a shared knock threshold the first player wins 31% and the last 21%, because whoever reaches a light lodge first can knock with a turn in hand. The match's rotating first seat evens this out over four rounds.

The sim's caveats: the bots don't count the registry beyond each shown face, never misremember, and judge the knock from perfect estimates. Humans will be slower to knock, so real rounds should run longer than 2 turns each.

### Open questions for the first table

1. **Is a round too short?** The sim says skilled rounds last about 2 turns each, or 8 turns at 4 players. That may feel like a sprint, or like the round ended before anything happened. If it is too short, raise the bar for knocking (for example, *you may only knock once your lodge shows no sign above Key*) rather than adding cards.
2. **Is the Turn fun or cruel?** A Turn on a placed card can swing a lodge by up to 8 points. That might be the moment the table remembers, or a take-that that makes keeping feel pointless.
3. **Does counting matter, or is it Beverbende with extra steps?** If players never use the shown faces or the registry to reason about rival cards, the deck isn't earning its place.
4. **Does the first-seat edge show up?** Watch it over a full four-round match.
