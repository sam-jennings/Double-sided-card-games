# Six players, four of them French, one deck, one slot

*Plan for a board game afternoon, August 2026 · Sam + 5 · WILDFIRE then BLIGHT*

**WILDFIRE first as the warm-up, BLIGHT as the main event.** One printed sheet in
French per pair of players; the games themselves are taught by playing them.

---

## Why this pairing works

BLIGHT is the right instinct. It is your only **Proven** game, it takes exactly
six, and at six players the hand is **6 cards** — which is precisely the open
question its playtest left behind (*"at 4P (9) / 6P (6) does the hand-handling
load drop to comfortable?"*). You'd be answering it.

It also turns out that the language barrier *helps* BLIGHT in one specific way.
The rulebook's biggest teaching warning is that players used to suit-and-rank
decks miss that **sign is rank** — that Crown is always 9. Four players who
can't read the word CROWN on the card won't make that mistake: they'll read the
number in the corner, which is the authoritative rank anyway.

And WILDFIRE isn't just a filler in front of it. It teaches, in ten silent
minutes, the exact three things BLIGHT depends on: that a card has two faces,
that you rotate it in your hand to find the face you need, and that the face you
*aren't* showing is doing something. Going into BLIGHT cold, you'd have to teach
all three with words.

| Also plays 6 | Why not today |
|---|---|
| BRAVADO, THE COUNCIL, FORKED TONGUE, SLEEPER | All run on talking — claiming, lobbying, bluffing aloud. Across a language barrier they aren't hard, they're impossible. All four are In development anyway. |
| GLEAN | Same engine as BLIGHT, and good — but nine simultaneous sign-majorities is a scoring teach you cannot do without language. BLIGHT's scoring is "add up the numbers in front of you". |
| FAVOURS | Kept as the encore (sheet page 3). Teaches by pointing, but only 2 rounds at 6P and ~55% of games tie. |

## The honest time problem

You said one slot, 20–30 minutes. Two games is **~32 minutes** at best, and that
assumes the teach goes cleanly. So: one race of WILDFIRE, not two, and **one
round of BLIGHT, not the full match**.

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Ask. Hand out sheets face down; don't let anyone read yet. |
| 2–5 | WILDFIRE setup + silent teach. |
| 5–7 | Practice lap, all hands face up. |
| 7–13 | **WILDFIRE race.** |
| 13–14 | **Decision point** (below). |
| 14–18 | BLIGHT deal + silent teach + one open practice trick. |
| 18–30 | **BLIGHT, one round of 6 tricks.** Score it. |
| 30–32 | Announce the lowest total. Stop there. |

**The decision point at minute 13.** If the WILDFIRE race ended with people
laughing, reaching for the deck, or asking for another — go to BLIGHT. If it
ended flat, confused, or politely, **don't escalate**: run a second WILDFIRE
race instead and finish. A second race of a game they half-understood is a
pleasure; a medium-weight trick-taker taught to a table that's already lost is
twenty minutes of everyone being kind to you.

**Do not attempt the full BLIGHT match** (one round per player = six rounds).
One round, scored, lowest total wins, done.

---

## Silent teach — WILDFIRE

Do all of this *doing*, not describing. Your hands are the rulebook.

1. **Hold up one card. Turn it over. Hold it up again.** Fan four or five cards
   showing both sides. Let it land that there is no back.
2. **Deal 5 to each player, face up in front of them** for the practice lap.
   Flip two cards to the middle, spaced apart. Tap each twice — two targets.
3. **Stack the last 4 face up to the side.** Point at it, mime taking two into
   your hand. That's the whole refusal rule.
4. **Play the first turn yourself, exaggerated.** Look at both targets. Rotate a
   card in your hand to show its other face. Place it so the matching symbol
   shows — hold it there, pointing between the two identical symbols. Then
   **turn it over in place**, point at the new symbol, then at the next player.
5. **Play the next player's turn for them in gesture** — hand them the card,
   guide it down, flip it. Let them do the one after alone.
6. **Go round once.** When someone can't play, mime the two-card draw. If two
   piles happen to match, demonstrate Mirror; if it doesn't happen naturally,
   engineer it on your own turn — point at the matching pair, point left, cross
   your arms, then have that player turn a pile over.
7. **Everyone picks their hand up. Race begins.**

## Silent teach — BLIGHT

The one concept that **must** land before the first trick is that
**winning a trick is bad**. Every trick-taking instinct anyone has is inverted,
and you can't explain it. So perform it.

1. **Deal 6 each.** Let everyone turn their cards over and study both faces.
   Point at the corner number on a few faces, then hold two cards side by side —
   9 next to 1 — and tilt your hand like a scale. The number is the strength.
2. **First trick with all hands face up on the table.** This costs ninety
   seconds and prevents every following error.
3. **Lead a card.** Slap the table, point at the sign you're showing — this sign.
4. **Go round pointing at each player, then at your led sign.** When someone
   hesitates, take their hand and turn one of their cards over to reveal the led
   sign on its back — *that* is the move nobody discovers on their own.
5. **Someone genuinely can't follow:** wave a flat hand, have them play anything,
   then push their card slightly out of the trick — sloughs can't win.
6. **The reveal.** Say "trois, deux, un" and everyone flips their own played card
   at once. Make this a ritual; it needs no words and it's the best moment in the
   game.
7. **Point at each revealed number. Point at the biggest. Point at that player —
   then groan.** Push the cards toward them with visible reluctance. Grimace.
   If it's you who wins, put your head in your hands.
8. **They keep the trick face-up, spread, exactly as revealed.** Point at their
   numbers, mime adding, thumb down. Then point at an empty space in front of
   someone else and thumb up. Lowest pile wins.
9. **Play one more open trick, then everyone picks their hand up.**

### Say these, and nothing else

- **The ask:** *« J'ai inventé quelques jeux avec ce paquet. Il y en a deux qui
  se jouent à six et presque sans parler. Ça vous dirait d'essayer ? C'est un
  prototype — vos critiques me sont plus utiles que la partie. »*
- **WILDFIRE, if someone stalls:** *« Une carte, sur l'un des deux feux, même
  signe. Puis tu la retournes. »* / *« Tu ne peux pas ? Prends deux cartes. »* /
  *« Il te reste une carte — dis Wildfire ! »*
- **BLIGHT, the whole game in two sentences:** *« Tu dois suivre le signe demandé
  — regarde tes deux faces. Le plus grand chiffre caché remporte le pli, et
  remporter un pli, c'est mauvais. »*
- **BLIGHT, when someone looks pleased to have won a trick:** *« Non non —
  gagner, c'est perdre ici. Le plus petit total gagne. »*

"WILDFIRE" is a proper noun. Everyone can shout it in any language. That is the
only word the first game needs.

## Five things that will bite you — rehearse them first

1. **WILDFIRE's well is only 4 cards at six players.** Two refusals empty it, so
   the wordiest rule in the game (lift the newest card off the taller pile,
   gather the rest of that line, flip the stack, square it as the new well, lay
   the newest card back, targets unchanged) will very likely fire in the first
   three minutes. **Practise it alone twice before you go** so you can perform it
   silently in ten seconds.
2. **Don't police WILDFIRE's announcement penalty.** "Forgot to say Wildfire →
   take 2" needs a shared language to argue about. Let it slide.
3. **Keep WILDFIRE's piles as loose overlapping lines.** People square card piles
   by instinct, and every time they do, the game's public memory vanishes. Nudge
   them apart with your hand; no words needed.
4. **BLIGHT's must-follow rule will be broken by accident, repeatedly**, because
   new players don't check the backs of their own cards. An illegal slough is
   normally exposed at the reveal — which here becomes an accusation nobody can
   discuss. **Allow silent take-backs all round, no penalties.** Just turn the
   card over, point at the led sign, and smile.
5. **BLIGHT's rot piles must never be tidied or turned over.** The whole
   no-memory design rests on them sitting penalty-face-up. If someone stacks
   theirs neatly, spread it back out.

## What to watch (you cannot ask, so don't plan to)

**WILDFIRE — the v1.2 open question:** *does anyone choose a card because of the
target it leaves?* You'll see it, not hear it — a card hovering over one pile
then moving to the other before they let go; a glance at their left-hand
neighbour before committing; eyes going pile, pile, hand, pile hunting the
Mirror card.

**BLIGHT — the questions its first playtest left open:**

- **Hand comfort at 6 cards.** This is the real prize. At 3P the 12-card hand was
  a logged bug (cross-game F1: no natural sort, both faces, realign every trick).
  Six cards should be fine. Watch hands, not faces — are people fanning
  comfortably, or laying cards on the table because they can't hold them?
- **Does ducking create satisfying agony, or passive dumping?** Watch whether
  anyone *thinks* before shedding. If cards get thrown out instantly, the craft
  isn't landing.
- **Does a surprise hidden 9 read as drama or a gotcha?** This is the one most at
  risk today. A gotcha you can laugh about in a shared language can just sting
  in a room where you can't. Watch the face of whoever eats the first big one.
- **Did anyone read the French sheet unaided and get it?** Lauren did that with
  the English rulebook and it was the standout of that session. The sheet is the
  equivalent test.

**Read the result honestly afterwards.** At 6P BLIGHT's skill gap is 1.41× — its
thinnest, deliberately the lighter, higher-variance configuration. And one round
does not wash out hand luck. So if someone gets a bad hand and finishes last,
that is **not** evidence about the game, and a strong or weak result today should
not move BLIGHT's status. What today can genuinely settle is handling and
teachability.

**The real verdict is behavioural.** If they ask for the next round before you
offer it, the games work. If someone picks up the deck afterwards and starts
turning cards over on their own, you have something.

## If there's more time, or if it sags

**They want more:** FAVOURS at six (sheet page 3). The only game in the
collection that gets *better* with a language barrier — every card names two
people at the table, so it teaches by pointing, and Silent FAVOURS is the
default rather than a compromise. Two deals, ~10 min. The 6-player removal is not
optional: take out **1–6, 2–5, 3–4** exactly.

**WILDFIRE felt slow:** run the second race as **Inferno** — two cards per turn.
Show it by playing two on your own turn and holding up two fingers.

**BLIGHT confuses the table:** stop, all hands face up, play two open tricks.
Open hands fixes almost everything and costs ninety seconds. If it's still not
landing, switch to **Only Crowns Bite** — point at the eight Crown faces, and
only those count. Fewer numbers, same game.

**Someone's clearly not enjoying it:** hand them the deck as an object. Every
pair of nine signs, exactly once; lay them out in rows by their lower sign and a
missing card names itself. A two-minute party trick that needs no language, and
it ends on the deck rather than on a game.
