# THE COUNCIL — Playtest Plan 01

*Pairs with the forthcoming `COUNCIL_playtest_01.md`. Written per `.kiro/steering/playtesting.md`. The hypotheses come from the rulebook's design footnote (first playtest questions), the overview's test focus (kingmaking at 5–6P; stack arithmetic staying frictionless) and [COUNCIL_design_analysis.md](COUNCIL_design_analysis.md).*

## Open before the session

No rule gap blocks play. Before tabling, the designer should confirm two v1.0a clarifications (see the analysis §1):

- **Show your alternative.** Committed cards' other faces are shown after the slap. This makes the rulebook's stated "the threat is public" true.
- **Issue face.** The underside of the turned-over card is the issue.

If the designer reverses either, edit the rulebook before the session, not during it.

## Setup

- **Game:** THE COUNCIL
- **Version:** v1.0a (vetted; floor-simulated over 60,000 games; never played).
- **Players:** **4** is the target. Three issues against four players forces a clash or an abstention every round, which is the game's premise. 3P is an acceptable fallback, and 5–6P is a later session for the kingmaking question.
- **Symbol subset:** all 9 signs / 36 cards. Hands of 4, issue deck 20 (12 used).
- **Session shape:** two full games (4 rounds each), the Speaker rotation restarting from a different seat in game 2.
- **Why this session:** the only collection game of open negotiation with reversible public promises. Its value is entirely social, so only a table can judge it.

## Hypotheses to test

- **H1 — The minority game is real.** *Prediction:* in most rounds at least one player talks someone off an issue, or into the cheap seats, and the table's talk visibly changes at least one commitment per game. *Falsified if* players commit silently and the talk phase is skipped or perfunctory.
- **H2 — Deadlocks roll often enough to matter.** The sim predicts ~2 open stacks after each round and ~20 pips rotting per game. *Prediction:* by round 3–4 at least one stack of two or more cards is on the table, and its fate draws the round's longest argument. *Falsified if* stacks are almost always claimed at once (no build-up), or so many rot that players call the scoring arbitrary.
- **H3 — The turncoat phase produces moments.** *Prediction:* at least one flip per game draws a groan or laugh, and at least once a player declines a public threat to flip, and the table reacts to the non-flip. The sim flips about once per game with careful play. *Falsified if* the phase is routinely "pass, pass, pass".
- **H4 — Flip order is fair enough.** Sim seat spread is within ±2 points at every count. *Prediction:* no player complains that their turncoat position decided a round more than once a game. *Falsified if* the last seat to flip (the Speaker) regularly takes the biggest stack by reacting.
- **H5 — Four rounds is the right length.** *Prediction:* the game ends with the table wanting one more round, not relieved. *Falsified if* players say round 4 is the first interesting round (too short), or it drags.
- **H6 — Stack arithmetic stays frictionless.** *Prediction:* stack values (pip × cards, as a cascade) are read at a glance, and the final count takes under a minute. *Falsified if* players repeatedly recount stacks or argue over totals.

## What to watch

Handling first:

- **Self-deal and hand holding.** Watch for fans tipping their outer faces to neighbours. Note any leak, and whether it mattered.
- **Palms commit.** Watch for a card flashing during the slap, or players committing out of sync.
- **Show your alternative.** Does it feel like a natural beat, or a chore? Do players ask to see an alternative again, and how often?
- **Issue tabling.** Is "turn it over, the underside is the issue" done consistently? Watch for a card laid the wrong way up.
- **Stack cascades.** Do they stay readable as they grow, and do cards get knocked out of their cascades?
- **Discard row.** Is it used to count what is left in hands (strategy note), or ignored?

Then:

- **Talk.** Time the talk phase per round. Note who leads it, and whether quieter players are steamrolled.
- **Kingmaking.** Does a player out of contention decide who wins? Note the round and the move.
- **Collusion.** At 3P (if used), does "everyone takes one issue each" appear?

## Observable success / failure signals

| | Holds (success) | Fails |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Talk changes commitments; deals made and broken | Silent commits; talk skipped |
| H2 | A multi-card stack builds and drives the climax | Instant claims every round, or arbitrary-feeling rot |
| H3 | Memorable flips and memorable non-flips | "Pass, pass, pass" |
| H4 | No repeated seat-order complaints | The Speaker routinely snipes the big stack |
| H5 | "One more round" | Round 4 is the only interesting one, or it drags |
| H6 | Stacks read at a glance; quick count | Recounts and arguments |
| Handling | No hand leaks; alternatives shown smoothly; issue faces consistent | Repeated leaks, forgotten alternatives, wrong-way issues |

## Teaching plan

- Say the rank canon (printed in Components) aloud.
- One-sentence core: "Three issues come up; everyone secretly commits a card; anyone may then defect to the one other sign on their card; an issue goes to whoever stands *alone* on it."
- Walk one demonstration round with open hands, including a deadlock that rolls over and a flip, before dealing for real.
- Stress two points: **nothing said is binding**, and **standing alone is the only way to win an issue**.
- Hold the strategy notes back until after game 1.
- **Watch during teaching:** whether "lone supporter takes all" needs repeating (players from majority games will expect the *most* supporters to win), and whether "abstention" is understood as a tactic or a mistake.

## Out of scope

- **5–6P kingmaking and the Speaker rotation at 5–6P.** A later session once 4P works.
- **Variants** (Backroom, The Whip, Long Parliament). Only after the base game has been played.
- **Re-simulation.** The card skeleton is certified; the open questions are social.
