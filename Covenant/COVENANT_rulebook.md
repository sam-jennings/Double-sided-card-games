# COVENANT

*A solo campaign of six chapters, with up to 3 optional assistants · ~5–15 minutes per chapter · ages 10+*

*Version A — The Loyalty Duel.*

**Covenant** (*KUV-uh-nunt*): a binding promise. You are **the Tenth** — a tenth sign with no face of your own — and the nine signs do not trust you yet. One chapter at a time, each new sign sends its people to test you. They arrive all wearing the same face, and each one is secretly bound to one of your friends. Name the friend behind each stranger and the stranger turns, showing a friend's face, and joins you. Win all nine signs and the deck in your box is whole.

The deck in your box *is* your progress. You never write anything down: the signs you have won are the cards you play with.

---

## Components

- **36 double-faced cards.** There are 9 signs. Every possible *pair* of two different signs appears on exactly one card (one sign per face), so each sign appears on exactly 8 cards. Every card shows its sign's pip (1–9) on both faces, so sorting by sign is quick.
- This rulebook. Nothing else: no tokens, no pencil, no booklet.

The nine signs, in the order they join you:

| Pip | Sign | Joins in | Role in this game |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | 🌙 Moon | Founding | An elder founder |
| 2 | 🍃 Leaf | Founding | An elder founder |
| 3 | 🌊 Wave | Founding | An elder founder |
| 4 | 🔥 Flame | Chapter 1 | The founder who tests you |
| 5 | 👁 Eye | Chapter 2 | |
| 6 | 🎭 Mask | Chapter 3 | |
| 7 | 🗝 Key | Chapter 4 | |
| 8 | ⭐ Star | Chapter 5 | |
| 9 | 👑 Crown | Chapter 6 | The finale. The Crown keeps your **ledger** until then. |

## The words in this book

- **Your covenant** — the signs you have already won. In chapter one that is Moon, Leaf and Wave. Your **covenant cards** are the cards whose *both* signs are in your covenant.
- **The newcomer** — the sign testing you this chapter.
- **Strangers** — the newcomer's cards that pair it with one of your covenant signs. There is exactly one stranger for each sign in your covenant (every pair exists once), so every stranger is bound to a *different* one of your friends.
- **Masked** — a stranger lying newcomer-face up. All masked strangers look identical. The face pressed against the table is its **true sign**.
- **Unmasked** — a stranger flipped to show its true sign, but not yet won.
- **Turned** — a stranger you have won.
- **Hand** — your covenant cards in front of you. Nothing in your hand is hidden: look at both faces of any hand card whenever you like.
- **Reserve** — your covenant cards not yet in hand, in a squared stack. You only take cards from it with powers.
- **Spent** pile — cards used for powers. Upright, any face showing.
- **Ash** pile — cards lost to wrong claims. Kept **sideways** (rotated 90°) so it can never be mistaken for the reserve.
- **Bank** — turned strangers, friend-face up.

## Campaign setup (once)

1. Sort the deck by sign into three stacks:
   - **The founders' cards:** the 6 cards whose two signs are both Moon, Leaf, Wave or Flame.
   - **The Crown's ledger:** the 8 cards that show Crown. Keep them squared together.
   - **The waiting cards:** everything else (22 cards).
2. Put the ledger and the waiting cards back in the box. Play chapter one with the founders' cards.

Your progress lives in the box between sessions: **the sub-deck you have won** (every card whose two signs are both won), the waiting cards, and the ledger. To find your place, look at the highest sign in your sub-deck — the next sign up is your next newcomer.

## Chapter setup

| Chapter | Newcomer | Your covenant | Strangers | Covenant cards | Opening hand | Reserve | Powers you can use |
|---:|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | Flame | Moon, Leaf, Wave | 3 | 3 | 3 (all) | 0 | none |
| 2 | Eye | signs 1–4 | 4 | 6 | 4 | 2 | Moon, Leaf, Wave, Flame |
| 3 | Mask | signs 1–5 | 5 | 10 | 5 | 5 | … + Eye |
| 4 | Key | signs 1–6 | 6 | 15 | 6 | 9 | … + Mask |
| 5 | Star | signs 1–7 | 7 | 21 | 7 | 14 | … + Key |
| 6 | Crown | signs 1–8 | 8 | 28 | 8 | 20 | … + Star |

Your opening hand is always **one card per stranger**, minus one per grudge you carry (never fewer than 3; see *Grudges*).

1. **Find the strangers.** From your sub-deck (chapter one) or the box, take the newcomer's cards that pair it with a covenant sign — one per covenant sign. (Chapter one: Flame/Moon, Flame/Leaf, Flame/Wave. Chapter six: the whole Crown ledger.) You will see their other faces while you sort. That is fine; the wash takes that knowledge away.
2. **The wash.** Lay the strangers on the table **newcomer-face up** in a loose heap. With both palms flat, slide them around for ten seconds or so, **never lifting a card**, until you could not say which is which. Then line them up in a row, still newcomer-face up, without lifting. Every stranger now shows the same face. (With an assistant at the table, let them do the wash while you look away.)
3. **Your covenant cards.** Shuffle the covenant cards (which face is up does not matter). Deal your opening hand in front of you; square the rest beside you as the reserve. Its top card shows one face; that is public and harmless.
4. Leave room for the Spent pile, the sideways Ash pile, and the bank.

> **One standing rule:** a stranger is only ever turned over by a claim or a power. Never lift one to look. Solo play runs on your honour, as every solitaire does.

## Your turn

COVENANT has no rounds and no turn limit. Take one **action** at a time, until the chapter ends:

- **Claim** a stranger, or
- **Use a power** (from chapter two onward).

You never draw automatically. **Your hand is your strength**: only powers bring cards in from the reserve.

### Claiming

1. Choose a stranger (masked or unmasked) and a card from your hand.
2. Lay the card beside the stranger **showing the sign you name**. You may only name a sign shown on that card (Mask and Star bend this; see *Powers*).
3. Flip the stranger over in place.
   - **Hit** — it shows the sign you named. The stranger is **turned**: move it to your bank, friend-face up. Your claim card **returns to your hand**. A true friend vouched for you, and stays.
   - **Miss** — it shows a different sign. Leave it where it is, flipped: it is now **unmasked**, and everyone can see its true sign. Your claim card goes to **Ash**. Then **the stranger strikes back**: the top card of your reserve also goes to Ash (if the reserve is empty, nothing more happens).

An unmasked stranger is easy to win — claim it naming the sign it shows, with any card that bears that sign — provided you still hold one.

> **Worked example (chapter one).** Your hand is Moon/Leaf, Moon/Wave and Leaf/Wave. You claim the left stranger with Moon/Leaf, naming Moon. You flip it: it shows **Leaf**. Miss. Moon/Leaf goes to Ash, and the stranger stays unmasked, showing Leaf.
>
> Next you claim it with Leaf/Wave, naming Leaf: a sure hit. It moves to your bank and Leaf/Wave comes back to your hand. The two masked strangers left must be Moon and Wave — one each.
>
> Now the choice that matters. You could risk Moon/Wave on either of them, or risk Leaf/Wave naming Wave. Risk **Leaf/Wave**. If it misses, you still hold Moon/Wave, which can name *both* remaining signs, and you will win. Risk Moon/Wave and miss, and the last card in your hand can't name the sign you need. You lose the rite.

### The last stranger is never a guess

Every stranger is bound to a different covenant sign, so once all but one are turned or unmasked, the last masked stranger's true sign is whichever covenant sign hasn't appeared. Claim it with a card bearing that sign. It is a sure hit.

## Powers (chapter two onward)

From chapter two, **every sign in your covenant gives you a power**. To use one, **spend** a hand card bearing that sign: show it, resolve the power, and put the card on the Spent pile. Either face of the card may be showing on Spent. Using a power is your action. Spent cards are gone for the rest of the chapter.

Mask and Star are different: they are **always on** and change how a claim works. Using them takes no extra action and spends nothing. The claim card returns on a hit or goes to Ash on a miss, as usual.

| Sign | Power | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 🌙 Moon | **Recover** | Take any one card from your Ash into your hand. *(Never from Spent.)* |
| 🍃 Leaf | **Grow** | Take the top two cards of your reserve into your hand. |
| 🌊 Wave | **Sift** | Look at the top three cards of your reserve. Take one into your hand; put the others on the bottom of the reserve. |
| 🔥 Flame | **Burn** | Flip any stranger that isn't turned and set it aside, unclaimed; it counts as dealt with. **The newcomer will join as a grudge.** |
| 👁 Eye | **See** | Flip one masked stranger in place. It is now unmasked. Nothing goes to Ash. |
| 🎭 Mask | **Two faces** *(always on)* | A claim made with a card bearing Mask names **both** of that card's signs. It hits if either is the true sign. |
| 🗝 Key | **Open** | Turn one *unmasked* stranger into your bank without a matching card. |
| ⭐ Star | **Wild** *(always on)* | A card bearing Star may name **any one sign** when you claim with it. |
| 👑 Crown | — | The Crown joins last, so its power never comes into play in the solo campaign. |

A card bearing two power signs offers either power, not both. The Mask/Star card, when claiming, names *either* Mask and Star together *or* any one sign.

---

## How a chapter ends

- **Won.** Every stranger is turned or burned. Gather the bank and *all* your covenant cards (hand, reserve, Spent and Ash) and shuffle them together. This is your new sub-deck. The newcomer has joined, and the save is written. Winning the trial and writing the save are one gesture.
  - If you burned any stranger, the newcomer joins as a **grudge** (below).
- **Failed hearing.** Your hand is empty while strangers remain, or you concede (you may concede at any time). Choose:
  - **Retry** — rebuild the chapter from setup and play it again. As often as you like.
  - **Accept a grudge** — the newcomer joins anyway, as a grudge.

**Score each won chapter** by counting your Ash pile *before* you shuffle: that is how many cards your trust cost you. Lower is better.

---

## Grudges

The story remembers how you won.

- **Recording a grudge.** Turn the newcomer's Crown card (Crown/newcomer) **sideways** in the ledger. Before the finale, the ledger cards are never played, so the record stays put. In chapter six, read the ledger, then straighten its cards and wash them as the Crown's strangers.
- **The toll.** In every later chapter, your opening hand is **one card smaller per grudge** you carry (never fewer than 3).
- A Crown grudge (accepted in the finale) has no toll to pay. It simply stands in your final record.

---

## The end of the campaign

When the Crown joins, all 36 cards are yours: the deck is whole. Record your **standing**:

| Total Ash across your six won chapters | Standing |
|---:|---|
| 24 or fewer | **Beloved** |
| 25–32 | **Trusted** |
| 33–40 | **Tolerated** |
| 41 or more | **Suffered** |

Each grudge drops your standing one step (below Suffered is **Endured**). These bands are provisional, set from simulated play: a careful player lands around 30, an unthinking one around 35.

## Assistants (optional, 2–4 players)

COVENANT is solo first. Up to three friends can sit in as **assistants**, so a group can play the campaign together without anyone owning more of it.

- At the start of each chapter from chapter two, each assistant chooses a different sign from your covenant to **embody**.
- Once per chapter, an assistant may **invoke** their sign's power for free, with your agreement. No card is spent, and it does not use up your action. Mask and Star assistants apply their effect to one claim of your choice.
- Assistants see everything you see. One of them should do the wash while you look away, so the order of the row is truly unknown to you.
- Assistants hold no cards and make no claims. The trial is still yours.

## Edge cases & FAQ

**Can I claim an unmasked stranger naming a sign it isn't showing?** You can, but it's a certain miss: the card goes to Ash and the stranger strikes back.

**Can I name a sign that's already turned or showing on an unmasked stranger, against a masked one?** You can, but it's a certain miss. Each covenant sign is bound to exactly one stranger.

**My hand has cards, but none can win anything.** Use a power if you have one (Leaf, Wave, Moon, Key). If not, the hearing is lost: concede, or claim anyway and watch the cards burn.

**The reserve is empty.** Leaf and Wave can't be used, and a miss costs only the claim card.

**Can Moon take back a card I spent on a power?** No. Moon only reaches into Ash.

**Can Eye or Key be used on a turned stranger?** No. Eye flips a *masked* stranger; Key turns an *unmasked* one.

**Is Flame worth it?** Sometimes. A burn saves a failing hearing at the price of a grudge, and you can always retry for free instead. That is your choice to make.

**Do I see the other face of my hand cards?** Yes, always. Nothing in your hand is hidden.

**The reserve's top card shows a sign. Is that a leak?** No. It only tells you one sign of the next card you might draw.

**Do grudged signs still give their powers?** Yes. A grudge costs you hand size, not the sign.

**What happens to the founders' cards in chapter one?** The 3 Flame cards are the strangers; the other 3 are your hand. Win, and all 6 shuffle together as your first sub-deck.

**I dropped the box.** Re-sort by sign. Your sub-deck is "every card whose two signs are both at or below your highest won sign". Sideways ledger cards are your grudges. If the ledger spilled, you will have to remember your grudges honestly.

## Chapter one: the founders' rite

Chapter one is a **teaching rite**. It takes about two minutes, and you have no powers. Be warned: it is a lesson, not a challenge. The rite contains exactly one real decision: after a miss, which card do you risk next? Play it well and you win 5 rites in 6. Play it carelessly and you win only 2 in 3. Play it once to learn the claim, the unmasking and the strike-back. The campaign starts to bite in chapter two, when the powers arrive and the reserve becomes something to protect.

---

## Strategy notes (read after your first chapter)

- **Claim what is unmasked first.** It's free: the card comes back.
- **Risk the redundant card.** Before you gamble, ask which signs are still in doubt. Risk the card whose loss leaves those signs covered.
- **Count down to the free claim.** The last masked stranger is never a guess.
- **Leaf early.** Leaf turns one card into two while the reserve is deep; the strike-back will eat the reserve anyway.
- **See before a long gamble.** With many masked strangers, a miss costs two cards. Eye costs one and never misses.
- **Mask cards are double chances.** Save them for the widest guesses.
- **Keep a Key for the stranger you can't match.** An unmasked stranger whose sign you no longer hold is a wall; Key walks through it.

## Designer's variants

- **Gentle rite** (first campaign, or with children): no strike-back. A miss costs only the claim card.
- **Strict hearings** (harder): each chapter allows three hearings. Fail the third, and the newcomer joins as a grudge.
- **Long memory** (a different challenge): after the wash, flip each stranger in place once, look, and flip it back before your first claim. Now the game is recall. *(This is the "Remembering" model from the design analysis, offered here as an alternative to try.)*

## Designer decisions taken in this draft

The concept, design analysis and trial-engine notes left these points open. Each is the simplest choice I could find that passes the physical-handling checklist. Each can be reversed.

1. **Engine.** Version A as the concept describes it: a uniform row, hidden partners, claims made with a hand card. One physical correction: a claim flips the stranger, so a **miss unmasks it**. The "information leak" is the honest flip, not a yes/no answer. The pure blind match (a 1.00× slot machine, July analysis), the memory model (now a variant) and the open-information "Turning" engine stay on file as alternatives.
2. **Chapter one** is Flame's trial against Moon, Leaf and Wave: 3 strangers and a 3-card hand from the 6 founder cards, with no powers. Six chapters in all; strangers number 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8.
3. **A hit returns the claim card to your hand; a miss sends it to Ash.**
4. **Strike-back:** a miss also ashes the top card of your reserve.
5. **Opening hand = one card per stranger, and no automatic draw.** A hand of 5 made chapters 5–6 almost unwinnable in simulation.
6. **Powers switch on from chapter two** for every covenant sign, the founders included.
7. **Powers adapted to a uniform row:** the concept's row-rearranging powers (Wave rearrange, Mask swap) do nothing when every stranger looks alike, and Key's "claim out of turn" means nothing solo. So:
   - Wave became **Sift**.
   - Mask became **Two faces** (always on).
   - Key became **Open** (win an unmasked stranger).
   - Eye **unmasks** publicly instead of a private peek, so nobody has to remember anything.
   - Moon recovers from **Ash only**, which guarantees every chapter ends.
   - Star stays the wild claim.
   - Crown has no campaign power.
8. **Flame Burn** flips the burned stranger and grudges the newcomer.
9. **Grudge effect:** a toll of −1 opening-hand card per grudge (minimum 3). This replaces the concept's per-sign "verb works against you", which had no defined effects.
10. **Grudge record:** the newcomer's Crown card turned sideways in the Crown's ledger. Strictly, this adds a second record alongside the sub-deck, but it lives in cards that are never played before the finale.
11. **Retries are free and unlimited** (concept). The limited version is the *Strict hearings* variant.
12. **Losing:** your hand is empty with strangers left. You may concede at any time.
13. **Score:** cards in Ash per won chapter. The standing bands are provisional, taken from the simulation.
14. **Assistants:** each embodies a different covenant sign and invokes it once per chapter, free and outside your action. They do the wash. The Model 4 "trial-master" mode is not in this draft.
15. **The wash:** slide flat, never lift. A stranger is only turned over by a claim or a power.
