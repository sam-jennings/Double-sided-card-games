# COVENANT — the deck that grows as the story does

*1 solo player (+ up to 3 assistants) · ~15 min per chapter · six-chapter campaign · sub-decks 6 → 36 · save = sub-deck composition · In development · physically unplayed*

> **Status & scope.** Story flight (July 2026), concept #28 — a campaign brief: progressive chapters, playable solo with optional assisting players, the signs integral to the narrative. Rubric waived, unsimmed, journal-derived. This record commits to **version A — The Loyalty Duel**, the permutation-trial engine; versions B (The Nine Rites anthology) and C (The Pilgrimage run engine) remain recorded alternatives in [NEW_GAME_CONCEPTS.md](../NEW_GAME_CONCEPTS.md) §28. Carries the two house disciplines: a physical-handling audit and a fastest-falsification test. It should not jump the [focus-priority.md](../.kiro/steering/focus-priority.md) queue on novelty alone.

## The trick that makes it exist

The [subset property](../.kiro/steering/deck-structure.md) is usually a player-count dial — declare which signs are in play and the remaining cards are an exact all-pairs deck. COVENANT turns that dial into a **campaign arc**. You are the Tenth, signless, earning each sign's trust in turn. The deck grows one sign at a time — 6 → 10 → 15 → 21 → 28 → 36 cards — and **the current sub-deck is the entire save file**: "which signs have I won" reconstructs the whole campaign from a shuffled box, with no writing, booklet, sleeve, or legacy stickers. Crown joins last, which is the house sub-deck order (Crown removed first) read backwards.

## Version A — The Loyalty Duel

**The founders and the joining order.** Chapter one is played with the four founder signs — Moon, Leaf, Wave, Flame (the 4-sign, 6-card sub-deck). Each later chapter admits one new sign in canonical order: Eye, then Mask, Key, Star, and finally Crown.

**The attack row (the gift the deck gives you).** In the chapter that admits sign *S*, take every card that pairs *S* with a sign you have already won and lay it *S*-face up. Because every card of a sign shows that sign's identical face, the row is **visually uniform** — every attacker looks the same. The only thing distinguishing them is the *hidden* partner face, and by pair-uniqueness those hidden faces are exactly one of each won sign. The trial is therefore a blind-matching permutation puzzle: claim each attacker by playing a card bearing its hidden sign from your covenant hand.

**Your deck is your health.** Wrong claims cost cards — the covenant deck *is* your health bar — but a wrong claim also leaks information for the next guess.

**Completion is the save.** Match every attacker and each matched card flips: the stranger's face turns to show a friend's, and shuffles into your deck. *Winning the trial and writing the save are one gesture.*

**Won signs are powers** (each spent by discarding a card bearing that sign):

| Sign | Power |
|---|---|
| Moon | Recover a spent card (catch-up) |
| Leaf | Draw |
| Wave | Rearrange the attack row |
| Flame | Remove an attacker unmatched — fast, but it joins as a **grudge** |
| Eye | Peek one hidden face |
| Mask | Swap two attackers |
| Key | Claim out of turn |
| Star | Answer as any sign (the wild) |
| Crown | Command an assistant's verb for free |

**Grudges.** Failure never blocks progress. Retry freely, or accept the sign as a grudge: it still joins the deck, but its verb also works *against* you for the rest of the campaign — the story remembers how you won.

**Chapter one.** The four founders test you themselves: a 6-card matching rite with zero powers that teaches the claim loop. The trial scales from 4 attackers (24 permutations) up to 8 attackers when Crown arrives.

**Solo + assists.** Each assisting player embodies one already-won sign and may invoke its verb once per chapter — helpers scale the game without owning it.

## How it uses the double-sided deck

- **The subset property as the save file.** No other component records progress; the set of signs in the box *is* the campaign state — the honest "sub-deck composition" save.
- **Every pair exactly once** makes the attack row a clean permutation: the hidden faces are guaranteed to be one of each won sign, so the puzzle is bounded and countable — deduction, not luck.
- **Uniform fronts, all-different backs** only exist because each card carries two different signs and no back. A generic deck cannot present that for free.
- **The flip is the reward**: matching flips an enemy face to a friend face, physically enacting the save.

## Handling audit

Pure sub-deck play; every mechanism is already sanctioned house practice (shuffle, rung-1 deals, dead/discard piles, the flip-on-match). No campaign components and no involuntary hidden-face bookkeeping — the attack row is a public, oriented registry, and the only concealment (an attacker's pressed partner) is legally deducible and verifiable by flipping on a claim.

## Open questions

- **Is the 6-card opening a game, or only a tutorial?** If chapter one is inert without powers, the arc may still hold, but the rulebook must say so honestly.
- **Does the blind-match stay tense as the row grows to 8?** Eight attackers plus peeking and swapping powers could tip from tension into bookkeeping.
- **Are grudges a real choice or an always-avoid trap?** The grudge must be tempting often enough to matter.
- **Do assistants add to the solo spine or just dilute it?**

## Fastest falsification

Play the 6-card opening chapter cold: is it a game at all, or does the fun only switch on at 15 cards? One solo sitting through two or three chapters answers whether the growing permutation puzzle earns its campaign framing.
