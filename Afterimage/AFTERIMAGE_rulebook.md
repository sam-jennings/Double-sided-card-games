# AFTERIMAGE

*A memory game for 2–4 players · ~10–15 minutes · ages 8+*

*An afterimage is what you still see after the thing itself has gone.* Every card on the table shows a sign. The moment a card is flipped, the sign it showed vanishes underneath, and only the players who were watching still see it. Find two cards hiding the same sign, take them, and keep going. When nothing is left to find, be the one who calls it.

---

## Components

- **36 double-faced cards.** There are 9 signs: Moon, Leaf, Wave, Flame, Eye, Mask, Key, Star and Crown. Every possible *pair* of two different signs appears on exactly one card, one sign per face. Each sign therefore appears on exactly 8 cards.
- This rulebook.

The cards have no backs. Whichever way a card lies, it shows one sign to everyone and presses its other sign against the table. In this game the pressed sign is what you are hunting for.

---

## Setup

1. **Wash-shuffle the deck.** Spread the cards on the table and swirl them, turning cards over as you go, so nobody knows which face of any card is up.
2. **Lay out the grid.** Place all 36 cards flat in a 6 × 6 grid with a little space between them. Each card shows one sign. Nobody may lift a card to look at the sign it presses.
3. **Choose a first player** at random. Play passes to the left.

Leave space in front of each player for the pairs they take.

---

## Your turn

On your turn, do **one** of these:

- **Guess:** flip two cards, looking for a match.
- **Sweep:** flip every card left in the grid to end the game. See [The Sweep](#the-sweep).

### A guess

1. **Flip a card.** Pick any card in the grid and turn it over **in place**. It now shows the sign it was pressing. Everyone sees it.
2. **Flip a second card.** Pick any other card and turn it over in place too.
3. **Compare the two signs that just appeared:**
   - **Match.** Both cards now show the same sign. Take both and set them in front of you. Each pair is worth **1 point**. Then **take another turn**: guess again, or sweep.
   - **Miss.** The signs differ. **Leave both cards exactly as they are**, showing their new signs. Do not turn them back. Your turn ends.

After a miss the table looks normal again: every card still shows a sign. But each of the two cards you flipped now presses the sign it showed a moment ago. Anyone who remembers what a card showed before it was flipped knows what it hides now. That memory is the game.

> **Example.** The grid has a card showing Leaf. You flip it and it shows Key. Your second card shows Moon when you flip it. That's a miss, so both cards stay put. The first card now shows Key and **presses Leaf**. Later, someone flips a card that turns up Leaf. If they remember the "Key" card used to show Leaf, they flip it next, and it turns up Leaf too: a match.

### Taken pairs

Keep your taken pairs face-up in front of you, matched sign showing. **Anyone may pick up any taken card at any time** to check its other face. Taken cards are public. They matter for counting (see [Counting pressed signs](#counting-pressed-signs-optional)).

---

## The Sweep

Matches always dry up before the grid is empty. The game usually stops with 2 to 8 cards left, each pressing a different sign. Ending the game is your call.

Instead of guessing, you may **sweep**: flip **every** card left in the grid, in place.

- **If no two cards now show the same sign**, the sweep succeeds. You score **1 point** and the game ends.
- **If any two cards show the same sign**, the sweep fails. Flip every card back again, in place, so each shows what it showed before the sweep. Your turn ends. You lose nothing, but everyone at the table has now seen every pressed sign.

**With fewer than 2 cards left in the grid, sweeping is your only move.** One card can't match anything, so that sweep always succeeds. The player who takes the last possible pair therefore gets another turn, sweeps, and scores the Sweep point as well.

You may guess instead of sweeping, even when you are sure the game is over.

---

## Game end and scoring

The game ends only after a successful sweep. Count your points:

- **1 point** for each pair you took.
- **1 point** for the successful sweep, if it was yours.

**Most points wins.**

**Ties:** if the player who made the successful sweep is among the tied players, they win. Otherwise the tied players share the win.

## Counting pressed signs (optional)

You never *have* to count. But the deck lets you, and it is the difference between a good player and a great one.

Each sign is on exactly 8 cards. So at any moment, the number of grid cards **pressing** a sign is:

> **8 − (grid cards showing that sign) − (taken cards with that sign on either face)**

Example: 2 grid cards show Crown, and the taken pairs include 3 Crown faces. Then 8 − 2 − 3 = **3** grid cards press Crown. You don't know which ones, but you know a Crown pair is still out there.

When every sign counts 0 or 1, no pair is left, and your sweep cannot fail. Counting tells you this before memory alone does.

Two more facts the deck guarantees:

- **A card showing a sign can't press the same sign.** Its two faces are always different. So if your first flip turns up Wave, never pick a card *showing* Wave as your second flip.
- **Two cards showing the same sign never match each other.** They are Wave–something and Wave–something-else, and those "somethings" are different signs.

## Edge cases and FAQ

**Can I flip a card that has already been flipped?** Yes. Any card in the grid is fair game, as either your first or second flip.

**Do I have to choose both cards before flipping?** No. Flip the first, look at what appears, then choose the second.

**Can I lift a card just to peek at what it presses?** No. The only ways to see a pressed sign are to flip the card as part of a guess or a sweep.

**Someone knocked a card and showed its pressed sign by accident.** Put it back the way it was, showing the same sign as before. Everyone who saw it may remember it. No penalty.

**May I take notes or rearrange the grid to help me remember?** No. Cards are flipped in place and never moved until taken.

**Can I ask someone what a card used to show?** No. Nobody may say aloud what any grid card used to show or now presses. Your memory is your own.

**What if my second flip is one of the cards from my last miss?** That's fine. It flips back to its old sign, which may or may not match your first flip.

**What if the grid is empty?** It can happen, but rarely: about 1 game in 80. Whoever took the last pair goes again, and with no cards left the sweep succeeds at once. They score the Sweep point and the game ends.

**Can a failed sweep give the next player a free pair?** Yes, often. Everyone has just seen every pressed sign, so the next player can take any pair they remember. That is the risk of sweeping too early.

**I'm losing and I know the game is over. Must I sweep?** No. You may make a guess instead. Pick your two cards carefully: a miss can leave a new pair pressed for the next player. If nobody finds a pair, the next player who is sure the game is over will sweep.

**Is it a match if a card I flip turns up a sign that another card is *showing*?** No. A match is only two cards that both turn up the same sign during the same guess.

## Strategy notes (read after your first game)

- **Remember the flip, not the card.** What matters is what a card showed *before* it was flipped. When a miss happens, say it to yourself: "the Key card was a Leaf".
- **Your second flip is where the skill is.** If you remember a card pressing the sign your first flip turned up, take it. If you don't, flip a card you know nothing about, and never one showing that sign.
- **Every miss is a gift to the next player.** Your two flipped cards now press signs everyone just saw. A careful first flip turns over a card whose *shown* sign isn't pressed anywhere else (count it), so your miss can't set up a pair for the next player.
- **Count before you sweep.** A failed sweep shows everyone every pressed sign and hands the table to the next player. A counted sweep can't fail.
- **The Sweep point breaks ties.** In a close game, being the one who knows the end has come is often worth the win.

## Variants

### First game: seven signs

For a first game, or for younger players, use only signs 1–7. **Remove every card showing Star or Crown on either face.** That leaves 21 cards, an exact seven-sign deck. Lay them in a 7 × 3 grid. Everything else is the same, except that each sign is on **6** cards, so counting starts from 6 instead of 8.

The game is about half as long. Simulated perfect players need a median of 16 guesses instead of 29, and a weaker memory has more of a chance.

### Marked flips

Whenever a card is flipped in a guess, leave it turned a quarter-turn sideways (a card that was already sideways stays sideways). The table can then see which cards have been seen, but not what they press. Turn every card back upright after a sweep is checked. Try one game with this and one without. It may make the game clearer for new players, or easier than it should be.

## Designer decisions taken in this draft

These were not settled in the concept. Each one can be reversed without touching the rest.

1. **Tiebreak:** the successful sweeper wins a tie they are part of; otherwise the tie is shared. In the sim this leaves 0% of 2-player games tied, 0–3% at 3 players and 0–6% at 4 players, with seats within 6 points of even. "Whoever took the most recent pair wins ties" was almost identical (0–5% shared), so the choice is about feel: the sweeper rule rewards counting.
2. **Taken pairs are public:** anyone may inspect any taken card at any time, so counting never depends on remembering taken cards.
3. **Cards flip in place and never move** until taken; no peeking, no notes, no rearranging. An accidental reveal is put back with no penalty.
4. **The last pair:** with fewer than 2 cards left, the only move is to sweep. So whoever takes the last possible pair also scores the Sweep point.
5. **A failed sweep** is flipped back in place and ends the turn, with no other penalty.
6. **You may always guess instead of sweeping,** even when you know the game is over.
7. **First-game variant** uses signs 1–7 (21 cards, 7 × 3 grid, counting from 6).
8. **Marked flips** (sideways cards) is a variant, not part of the base game.
9. **Grid:** 6 × 6, first player at random, play to the left.
10. **No memory talk:** nobody may say aloud what a grid card used to show or now presses.
