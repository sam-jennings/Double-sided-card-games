# EDICT — the law is on the table, the seal is in the lap

*3–6 players · ~25 min · induction (Eleusis-descended) · 9 signs, 36 cards · Stage 0 (concept)*

> **Status & scope.** Wildcard batch, first flight (July 2026). Written to a deliberately different brief: *maximum originality, rubric waived, no simulation required.* Playable as written, but no rulebook, no sim, no rubric self-score, and it should not jump the [focus-priority.md](../.kiro/steering/focus-priority.md) queue on novelty alone. Carries the two house disciplines: a full physical-handling audit and a fastest-falsification test. Portfolio index: [NEW_GAME_CONCEPTS.md](../NEW_GAME_CONCEPTS.md) §21.

**The trick that makes it exist.** An induction game — the genre where one player *is* the rules — which nothing in the collection touches. Everyone can read the 18 possible Laws (a printed menu); the **Keeper** has secretly enacted one; every card played is an experiment; and the table accumulates the whole lab notebook physically: accepted plays in the **Line**, rejected plays in the **Gutter** directly beneath the position where they failed, faces preserved. A backless deck makes the entire experimental record public, spatial, and re-readable — Eleusis with no scorepad and no memory.

**The seal (the deck-native commitment).** The Keeper takes the deck into their lap, selects one card, returns the rest. The seal is pressed to the table in front of them: **hidden pip = the Law's number; upright = Table A, sideways = Table B**; the shown face is noise. A secret written in the deck's own alphabet — the SLEEPER loyalty technique promoted to jurisprudence — unforgeable after the fact, verified at reveal.

**Deal.** Reserve 6 cards as an open stock (penalty draws); deal the rest as evenly as possible.

**Turn.** Either **play** one card to the Line's right end, choosing which face shows — every hand card is *two* possible experiments, so hand-reading is experiment design — or **accuse**: name a Law aloud. The Keeper rules each play *Lawful* (it stays) or *Unlawful* (it slides to the Gutter under that position; offender draws 1). A correct accusation flips the seal and wins; a wrong one costs 2 draws and kills that Law for everyone. First correct accuser wins; if the stock exhausts unguessed, nobody does.

**Keeper scoring (the god-must-be-knowable clause).** If the Law is eventually guessed, the Keeper scores the number of Unlawful plays it caused; if never guessed, the Keeper scores **0**. The Keeper's whole game is choosing, from a fixed menu, the law *this table* will find maximally confounding but still catchable.

**The menu (the real design work).** Two tables of nine, every law checkable from shown faces alone, each falsifiable within ~3 well-chosen plays. Table A = pip laws (odd/even must alternate; each pip differs from the last by ≥3; no pip repeats until three others intervene…). Table B = sign laws that only exist on K₉-with-almanac (consecutive signs must share a season-triangle; must *not* share one; high signs (6–9) and low (1–5) must alternate, Star exempt as wild…). The game is the menu; the menu is where the first draft effort goes.

**Honest note (rubric waived, conscience intact).** Induction *could* run on an ordinary deck. What is native here: the lap-seal commitment, the two-faces-per-card experiment choice, the Gutter as a backless public archive, and the whole of Table B.

**Fastest falsification.** One 4P round with a draft menu: if the Law falls inside four turns, or survives a whole deal for two Keepers running, the menu's difficulty band is wrong — iterate the menu, not the engine.
