# STRATA — every lie is buried where you left it

*3–5 players · ~20 min · bluffing / deliberate memory / shedding race · full 36 · In development · physically unplayed*

> **Status & scope.** Wildcard batch, first flight (July 2026). Written to a deliberately different brief: *maximum originality, rubric waived, no simulation required.* Playable as written, but no rulebook, no sim, no rubric self-score, and it should not jump the [focus-priority.md](../.kiro/steering/focus-priority.md) queue on novelty alone. Carries the two house disciplines: a full physical-handling audit and a fastest-falsification test. Portfolio index: [NEW_GAME_CONCEPTS.md](../NEW_GAME_CONCEPTS.md) §19.

**The trick that makes it exist.** A bluffing game in which no claim is ever spoken — the lie is **sedimented**. Placing a card face-down onto a pile *is* the claim ("the face I am pressing matches the face that was showing"); the audit is an **excavation**; and attribution needs no components because the orientation marker is identical on both faces — **a card pointed at your seat stays pointed at you through any flip**. The one-mark-two-faces rule, load-bearing at last. This is also the deliberate, verifiable, symmetric **memory game that [physical-handling.md](../.kiro/steering/physical-handling.md) explicitly flags as a gap** (ICARUS's Kestrel variant gestures at it; STRATA lives there).

**Deal.** 3P ×12 · 4P ×9 · 5P ×7 (the 36th card seeds one dig as bedrock). Three shared **digs** grow as offset columns — every card's shown face keeps a visible sliver forever, so the *shown* history stays public; only the *pressed* faces sink.

**Turn.** Play exactly one card onto any dig, pointing it at your own seat. The implicit claim: the face you pressed matches the sign that was showing beneath it — a **clean seam**. Say nothing. Your card's other face is the new top. Empty digs are free (no claim). You must play every turn — and only three top signs exist, so a dry hand *forces* a bury. That, plus greed (see scoring), is why anyone lies.

**Fault-calling.** Before the next player plays, anyone may cry **"fault"** on the seam just laid. Lift and check. Broken: the layer takes the card back *plus* the exposed top card into hand. Clean: the false accuser **skips their next turn** — in a shedding race, tempo is the only currency, so the penalty needs no components.

**End & excavation.** When a player sheds their last card (+5 tempo bonus), everyone else takes one final turn. Then unwind each dig top-down, reading every seam and every signature: each clean seam scores its layer **+the buried pip**; each broken seam **−the buried pip**. Burying a Crown is nine points of nerve either way. Highest total wins.

**The skill braid.** Short-term memory (what was showing a moment ago — public until it was covered); the pair-registry count (once all eight Wave cards are accounted for, a seam claiming Wave is *provably* broken — lies become catchable by arithmetic as the game ages); and risk-shaping (bury big pips only where the sign-count is muddy).

**Handling audit.** Nothing repeatedly consulted is buried; the memory load is chosen, symmetric, and verifiable on demand (fault) and at end (excavation). The load-bearing physical question: do five seats around a table give five *legibly distinct* pointing angles in an offset column? If not, the signature needs a fallback (e.g. seat-edge alignment) — this is the first thing to test.

**Fastest falsification.** One 4P game: if fault-calling is constant (nobody can lie) or absent (nobody can remember), there is no sweet spot between the bluff and the memory, and the game dies.
