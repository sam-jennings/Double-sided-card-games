# BRAVADO

*for 3–6 players · 15–25 minutes · ages 8+*

*One player can see one side of the card. Everyone else can see the other. You must decide whether the sign you cannot see outranks the sign you can — and the only help you get is one word from a rival who already knows the answer and has no reason to be honest. Call it right and your streak grows. Bank it, or draw again and risk the lot.*

## Components

- The full master deck: **36 double-faced cards, 9 signs, every pair of signs on exactly one card, each sign on 8 cards.** No subset — BRAVADO uses all nine signs.
- **An opaque draw well.** The game box base is the house kit's sanctioned draw container ([DECK_CONVENTIONS](../Project/Deck/DECK_CONVENTIONS.md) rung 4); at home any opaque mug, bowl or bag around 9 cm deep works.
- **Round-win counters** — one per round won, from the shared 12-counter kit. Any coins or pebbles substitute; a scribbled tally is fine. (A long six-player match can want a thirteenth counter.)

The signs' fixed strengths 1–9 are used. Their per-game meanings and abilities are not.

> **Say aloud before the first game (rank canon):** "There are 9 signs. Each sign has one fixed strength — Moon is always 1, Crown is always 9, and the pip printed on every face is its rank. Every card pairs two *different* signs, so a card always has two different strengths, one per face."

## Objective

Win a round by banking the longest successful streak. **The first player to win three rounds wins the game.** For a shorter game, play exactly three rounds; most round wins wins.

## Setup

1. **Shuffle by flipping random chunks**, so that which face lands up is randomised. (There is no card back — every card always shows one of its two faces.)
2. Lay the whole deck flat inside the **draw well**, so no face is visible before a card is drawn. Draw by sliding a single card up the inside wall with your fingertips — never lift the stack out. Keep the well tilted away from the active player, or draw below the table line: the Caller is welcome to see what they are drawing, but the active player must not.
3. Choose a starting player. **Runs are taken one at a time, clockwise, beginning with the starting player.**
4. Clear a space in the centre for the **record** — an overlapping row of resolved cards, which stays readable by everyone all round.

## A player's run

On your run you begin with a streak of **zero**, and you may successfully call up to **six** cards.

**The Caller** is the player to your left as your run begins. The Caller changes as your run continues (see *Changing the Caller*).

### 1. The Caller presents a card

The Caller draws one card blind from the well and holds it upright between you and the rest of the table:

- **you** see one face;
- the **Caller and everyone else** see the opposite face.

You must not turn, tilt or touch the card.

> **Handling — check this in your first game.** The Caller holds the card **squared and vertical**, not tilted, and angled away from a window or overhead lamp so the card cannot be read through. Before the first real run, hold one card up and confirm two things: the active player genuinely cannot make out the far face, and the players sitting *beside* the active player genuinely can. Those seats have the shallowest angle, and the *Crowd* variant below depends on them seeing it.

### 2. Announce what you see

Say the sign and its number aloud — *"I see Flame, four."*

This must be truthful; the card will verify it in a moment. It is also the point of the step: **your announcement is what tells the Caller which card they are holding.** They can see one face and now know the other, so from here on the Caller — and the whole table — knows the answer, and you do not.

### 3. The Caller advises you

The Caller must say **exactly one word**: **"Higher"** or **"Lower."**

They may tell the truth or lie. Nothing forces either.

Everyone else may react, groan, laugh and hold whatever face they can manage, but **may not state the answer** or name a sign.

### 4. Make your call

Say **"Higher"** or **"Lower."** You may follow the Caller or contradict them.

Because a card's two faces always show **different** signs, a tie is impossible. There is always a right answer.

### 5. Reveal

The Caller lays the card in the centre so both faces can be inspected, then leaves it in the record row.

- **Correct** — the card joins your streak.
- **Wrong** — you **bust**. Your streak is worth **zero** and your run ends immediately.

Resolved cards stay in a public overlapping row, face up, and anyone may pick one up and read both sides at any time.

### After a correct call — bank or push

- **Bank.** End your run and record your streak: the number of cards in it.
- **Push.** Draw another card and go again.

Once you say Push you must resolve that card. You cannot change your mind after seeing your face.

A streak of **six** banks automatically — that is the cap.

## Changing the Caller

After every resolved card, the Caller role moves **one seat clockwise**. If it would land on the active player, skip them.

A long run therefore makes you read several different opponents instead of solving one person's bluffing pattern.

## Ending a round

Each player takes exactly one run, clockwise from the starting player.

The player who banked the **longest** streak wins the round and takes a counter. A busted streak counts as zero.

**Ties:** the player who banked that streak **first** wins it. Matching the current leader is not enough — later players must beat them.

Then pass the starting-player role one seat clockwise, return every card to the well, and reshuffle.

**If the well ever empties mid-run**, that run ends and the streak banks as it stands. (Six players each resolving the maximum six cards would use exactly all 36 cards, so there is no slack at that count — in practice busts mean this is very unlikely to come up.)

## Edge cases & FAQ

**Q. Can the Caller refuse to advise, or say anything other than one word?**
No. Exactly one word, every time: Higher or Lower. Silence and hedging are both illegal.

**Q. Can the Caller see the card before deciding what to say?**
Yes — the Caller sees their own face as they hold it up, and learns the other from your announcement. Advising from full knowledge is the whole role.

**Q. I announced the wrong sign by mistake.**
Correct it before the Caller speaks. Once the Caller has spoken, the announcement stands and the reveal settles it.

**Q. Three players. Does the Caller ever end up being me?**
No — the role skips the active player, so at three players it simply alternates between your two opponents.

**Q. Must I bank at six?**
Yes. Six is the cap and it banks automatically.

**Q. Can I bank without resolving any card?**
No. Every run resolves at least one card, and a zero-length streak is not a bank.

**Q. Someone is reading the record row during my turn.**
Allowed, at any time, by anyone — as long as cards are returned exactly as found and play isn't held hostage.

**Q. May the table discuss the odds out loud?**
Yes, freely. What they may not do is state the answer or name the hidden sign. (The *Stone Faces* variant below tightens this.)

## Why the decisions are interesting

### The pip you can see *is* the odds table

Every pair of signs exists exactly once, so a card showing sign **S** has its hidden face spread evenly across the eight signs it isn't:

> **P(higher) = (9 − S) ÷ 8**

| You see | Moon | Leaf | Wave | Flame | **Eye** | Mask | Key | Star | Crown |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| P(higher) | **100%** | 87.5% | 75% | 62.5% | **50%** | 37.5% | 25% | 12.5% | **0%** |

Nothing to memorise and nothing to look up — count how many signs sit above the one you can see. Moon and Crown are free calls and mostly comic relief; **Eye is a perfect coin flip**, and the middle cards are where the bluffing lives.

### The Caller cannot simply lie every time

A Caller who always lies is a Caller you invert. One who always tells the truth is one you follow. Either pattern hands you the game, so the Caller has to build a habit, break it, and trade on what you believe about them.

Conversely: **blindly following the Caller, or blindly contradicting them, are both losing lines** — each wins about a third of its fair share of rounds.

### Three is a trap

The tempting place to stop is three cards. It is the *worst* place to stop. Because you must beat the current leader outright — matching them loses the tie — a banked three is usually just a target for the seats behind you. Pushing to **five** is the strongest single policy, and a banked one is worth more than a banked three when the table busts. See [BRAVADO_design_analysis.md](BRAVADO_design_analysis.md) §3.

### The leader sets the pressure

A player who banks four forces everyone behind them to risk a fifth card. A weak early benchmark invites greed; a strong one turns every later run into a chase.

## Example run

Alex is active, with a streak of three. The Caller holds up a card; Alex sees **Leaf, two**.

The Caller says: *"Lower."*

Alex knows Lower is nearly impossible — only Moon is lower than Leaf, one chance in eight. So the Caller is almost certainly lying. Unless they are making an audacious *truthful* call precisely because it sounds like a lie.

Alex calls **Higher**.

The card is **Leaf / Moon**. Alex busts and loses all three cards.

The decision was statistically right, socially uncertain, and immediately dramatic — which is the whole game.

## First game

- Play to **two** round wins.
- Cap streaks at **five** cards.
- Allow free table talk once the Caller has spoken.

Once everyone has the structure, use the full six-card cap and restrict explicit advice to the Caller alone.

## Variants

**Stone Faces.** Until the call is made, only the Caller may speak or deliberately gesture. Everyone else stays neutral. Best for tables that enjoy reading physical tells.

**The Crowd.** After the Caller speaks, every other opponent simultaneously points up or down — honestly or not. Then the active player decides. Louder and more chaotic, strategically looser.

**Personal Challenge.** The active player picks their Caller before each draw instead of the role rotating. More socially aggressive, and it lets the same two players dominate a round, so rotation stays the default.

**Accomplice** *(untested — the designed repair if the advice falls flat).* When the active player **follows** the Caller's word and is **correct**, the Caller takes a counter from the kit as a **favour**. Three favours may be spent, at any time on your own run, to buy one extra push past the six-card cap. This gives the Caller a reason to *want* to be trusted, which is the one thing the base game does not supply. Switch to it if the table finds the Caller's word carries no information at all — see [BRAVADO_design_analysis.md](BRAVADO_design_analysis.md) §4.
